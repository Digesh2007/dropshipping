## 1. What is dropshipping?

Dropshipping is an order-fulfilment method. A seller lists products in an online store without keeping those products in their own warehouse. When a customer buys an item, the seller purchases or forwards the order to a supplier. The supplier packs and ships the product to the customer.

**The three main players**
- **Customer:** discovers the product, places an order and pays the seller.
- **You (the store owner):** choose products, create and manage the store, set prices, market products, handle customer service and manage refunds/complaints.
- **Supplier:** confirms stock, packs the item and dispatches it to the customer.

The seller is still responsible for the customer experience. Dropshipping does **not** remove responsibility for product quality, truthful advertising, delivery promises, consumer rights, tax compliance or refunds.

![How dropshipping works](images/how-dropshipping-works.png)

### Simple order flow

1. Customer orders from your online store and pays you.
2. You verify the order and send the order details to your supplier.
3. You pay the supplier the agreed wholesale/product and shipping cost.
4. Supplier packs and ships the product to the customer.
5. You share tracking updates and handle customer support.
6. Your remaining amount, after **all** costs, is your profit or loss.

**Important:** The customer normally buys from your store—not directly from your supplier. Make sure the supplier is authorised to fulfil orders and understands packaging, invoices, returns and delivery expectations.

## 2. How the business works

Example (illustrative numbers only):

| Item | Example amount |
|---|---:|
| Customer pays your store | ₹999 |
| Supplier product cost | ₹450 |
| Shipping charged by supplier | ₹80 |
| Payment/platform/transaction costs | ₹35 |
| Advertising cost allocated to this order | ₹180 |
| Refund/return/other cost allowance | ₹50 |
| **Estimated contribution before other overheads and taxes** | **₹204** |

Formula:

`Contribution = selling price − product cost − shipping − payment/platform fees − advertising − return/refund allowance − other variable costs`

Do not confuse revenue with profit. A ₹999 order does not mean you earned ₹999.

## 3. Indian vs international dropshipping

![Indian vs international dropshipping](images/india-vs-international.png)

| Topic | Selling within India | Selling to customers abroad |
|---|---|---|
| Customer market | Customers in India | Customers in selected foreign markets |
| Supplier | Often Indian wholesaler/manufacturer; may also be overseas | May be local to target country or in another country |
| Currency | Usually INR | May involve USD, EUR, GBP or local currency |
| Delivery | Domestic courier network | Cross-border or local fulfilment; depends on supplier location |
| Customs | Usually no import customs for a purely domestic shipment | Customs/import taxes may apply depending on route, product and destination |
| Payments | Indian payment methods such as UPI/cards, subject to gateway support | International cards/wallets and supported settlement currencies |
| Customer service | Local language, time zone and return expectations | Different time zones, languages, consumer rules and return logistics |
| Main challenges | Supplier reliability, COD/RTO where offered, competition, returns, GST | Longer or uncertain delivery, customs, duties, FX, chargebacks, destination-country rules |

### Which is easier to start with?

For a beginner based in India, a small domestic pilot can be easier to understand because the seller can focus on one market, one currency and local customer expectations. But it is not automatically low-risk or profitable.

International selling can open larger markets, but it adds complexity: destination-country taxes, customs, product restrictions, shipping, foreign exchange, payment acceptance and returns. Research one target country before launching; “international” is not one single market.

**Delivery times in the supplied infographic are examples, not guarantees.** Actual delivery depends on supplier location, dispatch time, courier, destination, customs and service level. Ask suppliers for real delivery data and test an order.

## 4. Types of dropshipping

![Types of dropshipping](images/types-of-dropshipping.png)

1. **General dropshipping:** products from multiple categories. Broad selection, but difficult to build a clear brand and focused marketing.
2. **Niche dropshipping:** products aimed at one category or audience, such as kitchen organisation or pet travel. Easier to create relevant content, but the niche still needs demand and margin validation.
3. **Private-label dropshipping:** products are sold under your own brand, with supplier-supported branding/packaging. Requires quality control, brand permissions, and often higher minimum quantities or setup costs.
4. **Wholesale-supplier fulfilment:** a wholesaler fulfils individual orders or agreed batches. Clarify whether the supplier supports one-by-one dispatch; not every wholesaler does.
5. **International dropshipping:** describes the market/fulfilment geography, not necessarily a separate fulfilment method. It can overlap with niche or private-label models.
6. **Digital products:** downloadable files, courses or software are not physical-product dropshipping in the usual sense. They are a separate digital-commerce model because there is no physical supplier shipment.

## 5. Documents, tools and compliance

![India dropshipping essentials](images/india-dropshipping-essentials.png)

### Do not teach “only PAN + phone + Wi-Fi” as a universal legal checklist

The documents and registrations depend on the seller's legal structure, sales channel, turnover, product, state, transaction route and whether goods cross India's border.

A practical **pre-launch checklist** may include:
- PAN and identity details of the proprietor/entity and authorised person.
- Business/contact address and address proof, as needed by the bank, payment provider, marketplace or registration process.
- Bank account details and KYC requested by the payment provider.
- Supplier agreement/order-fulfilment terms, supplier invoices and product documentation.
- Store policies: shipping, returns/refunds, privacy, terms, contact details and grievance/customer-support process.
- GST registration and tax setup **if applicable** to the business model and transactions.
- IEC and export/customs documents **if applicable** when exporting/importing goods from India.
- Product-specific approvals, labelling or certifications where required (for example, certain electronics, food, cosmetics or regulated goods).

A laptop is not a legal document and may not be strictly necessary for every task, but it is strongly useful for store setup, bookkeeping, product research and customer support. A reliable phone and internet connection are practical operating tools, not a substitute for compliance.

### GST caution for Indian sellers

Do not state that GST registration is never needed for dropshipping. GST treatment depends on the exact supply and sales channel. CBIC guidance says sellers supplying goods through an e-commerce operator required to collect TCS have registration obligations, while later government changes and specified schemes can create exceptions or alternative routes subject to conditions. Inter-State supply, exempt goods, direct website sales and marketplace sales can be treated differently.

**Action:** Before accepting orders, ask a qualified Indian GST practitioner to review your exact setup: who is the seller of record, where supplier and customer are located, who invoices whom, whether the platform is an e-commerce operator, and whether goods move between states.

### IEC / export-import caution

DGFT's Foreign Trade Policy describes IEC as generally required for import/export of goods unless specifically exempted. Export documentation and product rules can vary. If your supplier is overseas and ships directly to a customer in another country, establish who is importer/exporter of record and which country's customs/tax rules apply. Do not assume the Indian seller has no obligations just because the parcel never enters their home.

## 6. Costs, pricing and profit

Before choosing a product, calculate:

- Supplier's product price
- Shipping and fulfilment
- Packaging or branding fee
- Store subscription and apps
- Payment gateway and currency conversion fees
- Advertising and content creation
- Refunds, returns, damaged parcels and chargebacks
- Taxes and professional/accounting costs

### Product validation checklist

Score each candidate from 1–5 (5 is strongest):
- Is there a clear customer problem?
- Can you explain the benefit in a short video?
- Is demand visible in search/social marketplaces?
- Is the product easy to demonstrate?
- Is the supplier reliable and able to provide tracking?
- Is quality consistent?
- Is the product compact and not easily damaged?
- Is the margin still positive after ads, shipping and returns?
- Are there manageable safety, certification and regulatory requirements?
- Can you offer a useful difference rather than competing only on price?

Avoid making medical, safety or performance claims that you cannot prove. Order a sample before scaling.

## 7. Choosing a niche

A **niche** is a focused category or audience with a specific need. Examples:
- Kitchen organisation for small homes
- Pet travel accessories
- Desk organisation for students
- Travel organisation
- Home gardening accessories

A good niche is not simply “a trending product.” It should have:
1. A reachable audience.
2. A problem or desire people care about.
3. Several related products that can be sold together.
4. Suppliers with dependable quality and fulfilment.
5. Enough margin after every cost.
6. Manageable shipping, returns and compliance.

## 8. Example niche: kitchen organisation

**Proposed niche:** space-saving kitchen organisation for people living in apartments and small homes.

**Target audience:** renters, students, young professionals and families who want to organise limited kitchen space.

**Customer problem:** crowded cabinets, messy drawers, limited counter space and hard-to-reach items.

### Five example products to research

These are product ideas to validate—not guaranteed winners or confirmed supplier offers.

| # | Product idea | Problem it addresses | What to check before listing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Adjustable drawer divider / organiser | Cutlery and utensils become messy | Dimensions, material strength, adjustable fit |
| 2 | Under-sink sliding storage rack | Cleaning supplies waste cabinet space | Cabinet measurements, load capacity, rust resistance |
| 3 | Magnetic spice rack / fridge-side organiser | Spice jars take up counter space | Magnet strength, weight limit, jar compatibility |
| 4 | Airtight food-storage container set | Open packets create clutter and lose freshness | Food-contact material compliance, seal quality, capacity |
| 5 | Foldable dish-drying rack | Limited counter space near the sink | Drainage design, dimensions, rust resistance, stability |

### Content angles for this niche

- “5 ways to organise a small kitchen”
- “Before and after: one messy cabinet”
- “Does this under-sink rack fit your cabinet?”
- “Three mistakes when buying storage containers”
- “Small kitchen setup under a fixed budget”

### Niche validation mini-plan

1. Search the five product ideas on Indian marketplaces and social platforms.
2. Read recent low-star reviews to identify repeated complaints.
3. Compare at least three suppliers per product.
4. Ask suppliers for wholesale price, dispatch time, tracking, return handling, stock updates and sample availability.
5. Order samples of the strongest two products.
6. Calculate full contribution per order.
7. Publish helpful content and test demand before spending heavily on ads.
8. Launch only after quality, fulfilment and legal checks.

## 10. Quick quiz with answers

**Q1. In a typical dropshipping order, who ships the physical product?**  
A. Customer  B. Supplier  C. Payment gateway  
**Answer: B — Supplier.**

**Q2. What does the store owner mainly manage?**  
A. Store, marketing, orders and customer support  B. Customs in every country  C. Manufacturing every item  
**Answer: A.**

**Q3. Which statement is correct?**  
A. Sales revenue is always profit.  
B. Profit must account for product, shipping, marketing, fees, returns and other costs.  
**Answer: B.**

**Q4. What is a niche store?**  
A. A store focused on a specific category or audience.  
B. A store that sells every possible product.  
**Answer: A.**

A. Yes  B. No  
**Answer: B — No. Higher costs and complexity can reduce profit.**

**Q6. Is GST registration never needed for an Indian dropshipping seller?**

## 11. Practical TODO / homework

Complete this activity before choosing a product to sell.

- [ ] Choose one audience (e.g. students, apartment residents, pet owners).
- [ ] Identify one real problem that audience has.
- [ ] Pick one niche and explain why it is worth researching.
- [ ] List five related products (use the kitchen-organisation examples above or choose your own).
- [ ] Find at least three possible suppliers for each shortlisted product.
- [ ] Compare price, shipping, dispatch time, tracking, sample availability and return policy.
- [ ] Read at least 20 customer reviews per shortlisted product; note repeated complaints.
- [ ] Estimate selling price and every cost; calculate contribution per order.
- [ ] Check product safety, labelling, tax and other compliance requirements.
- [ ] Order a sample of the best two products.
- [ ] Write a clear delivery, return and refund policy.
- [ ] Decide whether to test in India first or target one named overseas country.
- [ ] Prepare one short product demonstration video and ask potential customers for feedback.

**Deliverable:** a one-page niche research sheet containing the audience, problem, five products, supplier comparison, estimated unit economics, risks and your go/no-go decision.

## 12. Glossary

- **Supplier:** business that provides the product and fulfils the order.
- **Fulfilment:** picking, packing and shipping an order.
- **Niche:** focused market category or audience.
- **AOV:** average order value.
- **COGS:** cost of goods sold; define exactly which direct costs you include.
- **Conversion rate:** percentage of visitors who complete a desired action, such as purchasing.
- **RTO:** return to origin; a parcel is returned without successful delivery.
- **Chargeback:** a card-payment dispute that may reverse a transaction.
- **IEC:** Importer-Exporter Code issued by DGFT for applicable import/export activity.
- **HS code:** product classification code used in customs and trade.
